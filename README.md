## Where RackNerd Falls Short (The Honest Cons)

I'm not going to pretend everything is perfect. Here's where it hurts.

**The bandwidth is generous but not unlimited.** 3 TB on the entry plan, scaling up to 20 TB on the 8 GB yearly. For most personal and small-business use that's plenty, but if you're serving heavy media or running a high-traffic site, you'll want to do the math before committing.

**Hardware is mid-tier, not bleeding edge.** These are Intel Xeon-based nodes with RAID-10 SSD storage. Solid and reliable, but if you specifically need NVMe Gen4 + DDR5 + Ryzen 7950X performance, the AMD line (when available, usually New York only) is what you want — and even then it's not always in stock.

**Los Angeles DC-02 is the most China-optimized location, and it sells out first.** If you're picking a server specifically for Asia-facing traffic, the popular configs in LA go out of stock during big sales. Set a reminder for restocks.

**No managed support on VPS by default.** You get root, you manage it. Fully managed support is available as an add-on, but the baseline VPS product assumes you know your way around a Linux shell. If you're coming from shared hosting and have never SSH'd into anything, there's a learning curve.

**Promo plans sometimes have limited location choice.** The cheapest configs on special promos may only be deployable in select datacenters. Read the order form carefully before checking out.

That's the whole flow. No surprise upsells, no waiting on a human to provision, no manual verification step.
